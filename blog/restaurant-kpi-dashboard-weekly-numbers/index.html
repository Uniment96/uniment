Restaurant KPI Dashboard: The 7 Numbers to Review Every Week

Most owners have more POS reports than they can read, and still cannot say whether it was a good week. These are the seven numbers that change decisions, and how to track them without a data team.

MR By Mayur Rane, founder of Uniment / 2 July 2026 / 7 min read

What is a restaurant KPI dashboard?

A restaurant KPI dashboard is one screen that shows your key numbers (sales, food cost %, channel mix and order trends) taken from your POS and stock data. You review the week in minutes instead of digging through spreadsheets.

The point is not more data. It is fewer, better decisions. A good weekly review answers three questions: did we make money, where did it come from, and what is quietly eating the margin?

Which KPIs should a restaurant review weekly?

Review these seven every week: sales vs last week, channel mix (dine-in, delivery, takeaway), food cost %, best and worst day, average order value per channel, promotion return, and waste in AED.

01 Sales vs last week

Look at the trend, not just the number. One week on its own tells you little. What matters is whether you are up or down on last week and on the same week last month. A rolling 7-day view smooths out one-off spikes like public holidays.

02 Channel mix: dine-in, delivery, takeaway

In the UAE, Talabat, Deliveroo and Noon Food can grow to 40 to 60% of orders, at very different margins from dine-in once commission is taken. If delivery share is rising but profit is not, channel mix is usually why. Track each channel’s share every week, not every quarter.

03 Food cost %

Food cost % is the number in your P&L you can control most. Check it weekly against a target, instead of finding out at month end when it is too late to act.

Example

A kitchen sitting at 34% food cost puts the exact number on the wall every Monday. Portioning tightens, one supplier gets renegotiated, and within a few weeks it is under 30%. Nothing clever happened. People just saw the number.

04 Best and worst day

Most owners know their busy day. Fewer know how much of the week it carries. If Friday turns out to be a third of your sales, that changes staffing, prep quantities and pricing. Know your worst day too: that is where a promotion does the most work.

05 Average order value per channel

One overall average order value hides more than it shows. A dine-in bill and a delivery basket behave differently. Track it per channel so you can see whether upsells, bundles or menu changes moved the number where you launched them.

06 Promotion return

Every discount costs you margin. Not every discount brings it back. For each promotion, compare the sales it brought in with the margin it gave away. Timing matters as much as the offer: a deal for regulars on your slowest evening usually earns more than the same deal on a Friday that was full anyway.

07 Waste in AED

A waste percentage is easy to ignore. Waste in dirhams is not. Log spoilage and over-prep every week and put the AED figure next to your sales. “AED 2,300 thrown away this week” changes kitchen habits faster than “4% waste”.

What is a good food cost percentage for a restaurant?

Most full-service and casual restaurants aim for food cost between 25% and 35% of sales. Where you should sit depends on your concept. Delivery-heavy kitchens need a tighter food cost to absorb app commissions.

The rhythm matters more than the target. A restaurant that checks food cost every week and reacts will beat one with a “better” target that it only checks once a month.

How often should you review restaurant KPIs?

Weekly. Same day, same time, 30 to 40 minutes. Daily checks are mostly noise, and monthly reviews find problems a month too late. Weekly is often enough to act and far enough apart to show real trends.

When the numbers are ready before the meeting, the meeting gets shorter. You stop spending it building the report and start spending it deciding what to change.

You do not need a data team or an integration project. Export last week’s sales from your POS (or use the spreadsheet you already keep), upload it to a restaurant tool like RevenueRoll, and you will have a working dashboard in about 10 minutes.
